Recalling an idea after a delay

After learning a concept, cover its definition and try to explain it in your own words. Then compare your explanation with the source. Answering first helps you tell the difference between a familiar phrase and a concept you can explain independently. If you missed a condition that matters, focus your next review on that condition.

Active recall describes how you practice. Spaced practice describes when you practice. You can combine them by explaining an idea today, trying again tomorrow, and checking it a few days later. If you can still explain the idea and its conditions, try a longer gap. If you cannot remember the key point, reread the source and try again after a shorter gap.

A useful exercise has an answer that can be checked. For example, why should you try answering before uncovering a definition? The answer should explain how trying first reveals the difference between recognizing the wording and being able to explain the idea yourself.
